The Challenge

Medication errors are a
design problem.

Ce projet de groupe a été réalisé dans le cadre d'un cours d'IHM. Suite aux retours du professeur, j'ai intégré ces commentaires en repensant et en concevant une interface de manière autonome.

Two core problems were identified through research into how patients interact with medications:

Problem 1: Some patients struggle to follow medical instructions — for medication schedules or appointment management — leading to missed doses or incorrect treatment.

Problem 2: In some situations, patients can become aggressive or at risk, which complicates care follow-up and endangers the care environment. Staff are not always trained to handle difficult situations.

User Research

Three personas,
three failure modes.

16 ans · Étudiant

Evan

Living alone to pursue his studies, Evan forgets medical appointments and doesn't always read dosage instructions — leading to medication errors.

27 ans · Project Manager

Julie

Busy and distracted, Julie forgets to put medications back in their boxes. Her children play with them. She often ends up with pills and no packaging or instructions.

75 ans · Retraité

Jean-Michel

Retired with Alzheimer's, living alone, he must prepare his medications without help — increasing the risk of errors in his daily treatment.

Brainstorming

Three concepts.
One selected.

✓ Selected

Dr.Bot

A physical robot designed to assist people with medication. It advises users, analyzes medications visually, and moves to bring help when needed.

Concept

Watch & Take

A touchscreen integrated into each medication box. Forces users to watch an explanatory video before opening — providing instructions and warnings.

Concept

SmartSight

Smart contact lenses capable of scanning medications and displaying precise dosage instructions directly in the user's field of vision.

Iteration 2

From robot to app:
MedScan.

After evaluating Dr.Bot, critical limitations emerged — hygiene concerns, accessibility for the hearing-impaired, self-medication risks, and high production costs. The team pivoted to a more feasible and scalable solution.

Dr.Bot — Evaluation

Why we pivoted

  • ⚠️ Accessibility issues for hearing-impaired users
  • ⚠️ Hygiene concerns with physical medication handling
  • ⚠️ Self-medication risks with an autonomous system
  • ⚠️ Expensive and complex to produce at scale

MedScan — App Features

A scalable alternative

  • Medication scanning (OCR / barcode)
  • Tablet and liquid identification
  • Accessible voice mode
  • Automatic synchronized reminders
  • Drug interaction detection
  • Stock tracking and renewal alerts
  • Public database identification
