The Problem

A full closet that
feels completely empty.

People have wardrobes full of clothes and still feel like they have nothing to wear. This isn't a scarcity problem — it's a cognitive one. Decision fatigue, choice overload, and wardrobe blindness conspire to make getting dressed harder than it should be.

Individuals who encounter a large number of options have difficulty making decisions, their satisfaction levels decrease, and they may postpone decisions entirely.

Iyengar & Lepper, 2000 · Schwartz, 2004

Problem 1 — The Combination Gap: Users struggle to remix what they own. When pressed for time, they default to familiar outfits rather than experimenting.

Problem 2 — Wardrobe Blindness: Users lose track of what they own. Items get buried, forgotten, or never worn again — leading to unnecessary purchases and a paradoxically empty-feeling closet.

How might we help users discover new outfit combinations from their existing wardrobe, so they spend less time searching and more time actually getting dressed?

My Process

From research to
a working model.

I built and trained a real AI compatibility scorer — not just a prototype concept, but a system trained on real clothing data.

01

Find & Test a Model

I selected OpenAI's CLIP model — a pre-trained vision-language model that understands clothing images and text descriptions. I tested it with a clothing item to confirm it could recognize garments, even with low confidence on ambiguous pieces.

02

Build a Dataset

I photographed clothing items against plain backgrounds, labeled each piece, and created outfit pairing spreadsheets — marking compatible combinations (1) and incompatible ones (0). Final dataset: 116 images, 210 outfit pairings.

03

Train the Compatibility Scorer

Images were converted into embeddings — numerical representations of each item's visual features. The outfit pairs spreadsheet was then used to train a compatibility model. 80% of data for training, 20% for testing.

04

Test & Evaluate

The model correctly predicted 35 out of 42 outfit combinations it had never seen before — an accuracy rate that validated the approach and opened the door to UI integration.

05

Design the UI

With the AI logic validated, I designed the full app experience in Figma — incorporating the brand palette and a tinder-style outfit approval flow that keeps the interaction playful and low-friction.

Key Insights

What the research revealed.

🤝

Trust is Critical

Only 41% of survey participants felt comfortable using an AI-powered outfit recommender — highlighting the importance of transparency and user control in the design.

📸

Photo Upload is a Friction Point

Requiring users to photograph their own wardrobe was identified as the biggest barrier to adoption. Future iterations should streamline or scaffold this step.

🔄

AI Improves Over Time

Recommendations become more accurate with user feedback and behavior data — the more someone uses the app, the more personalized the suggestions become.
